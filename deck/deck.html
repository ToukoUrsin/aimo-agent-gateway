3 · Over the limit

Nothing goes out before someone confirms.

  • "{{milk.ask}}" comes to {{milk.eur}}. This account may order {{milk.limit}} alone, so the cart stops.
  • The person who asked cannot approve it. Someone else says yes on their own phone.
  • Only then Vahvista tilaus (confirm order) works.

What it does not prove: in the prototype anyone who has the link can approve, and the typed name is not checked. Only the browser that asked is refused. In Minun Aimo it would be the signed-in user who has the right to approve.

/shop and the approval page, in two browsers, when this deck was built. The amounts and the limit are read from the page. "{{approver}}" was typed as the approver's name.

4 · Who decides

Valio Aimo would set the rules.

  • The AI reads the request and the cart. The program picks products, amounts and prices, under rules the AI cannot change.
  • Switches per customer. What may be ordered, a cap, a pause. The customer sets its own limits under those.
  • A rule passes, asks a person, or refuses. Nobody can approve what a rule refused.
  • An empty allergen field is not treated as safe. {{und.n}} of {{und.food}} food products in the challenge data ({{und.pct}}%) declare nothing about allergens. If the kitchen has an allergen rule, a person checks such a line.
Switches: /access. Allergen count: /proof, counted from the challenge's product data.

8 · Honestly

What we could not check

  • No cook has used it.
  • It runs on the challenge's mock API and product data. Nothing touches a Valio Aimo system.
  • Anyone who has the approval link can approve. The typed name is not checked.
  • The cook's words and the cart go to a hosted AI model (Google's Gemini). Valio Aimo would choose the model.

The way in

  • The chat goes into Minun Aimo for a few pilot kitchens.
  • That needs the Minun Aimo sign-in, the customer's own range and prices, and who may approve.
  • Outside software later, one customer at a time.
