Junction 2026 prototype on Valio Aimo's challenge data and mock API. Not a Valio Aimo system.

Architecture

For the people at Valio Aimo who own integrations and data governance: where this gateway would sit, what it keeps, what a customer's AI assistant can see through it, and what a pilot would need from you.

What this is

A gateway that lets a customer's AI assistant (Claude, ChatGPT, or the customer's own agent) look up products, get a signed offer and place an order with Valio Aimo, inside limits the customer and Valio Aimo both set. It is a prototype built at Junction 2026. It runs on the challenge's mock price and availability API and the challenge's product data file, with invented customers. It is not a Valio Aimo system and is connected to none: no ERP, no order management, no Peppol network. Everything below describes the code as it is, including what is missing. The demo itself happens in the Claude app, where our connector draws each step as a card in the chat (see /chat).

Where it sits

Three parts. On the buyer's side, an assistant and a second person with a phone. In the middle, the gateway: one door for assistants (MCP), one for programs (REST), a sign-in, the rules, the signer, the ledger and the approval page. Behind it, Valio Aimo's own systems, which in this prototype are the challenge's stand-ins.

All of the gateway is one Python process (FastAPI) with one SQLite file. MCP and REST call the same functions in gateway/service.py and return the same documents, so the rules cannot differ between the two doors. The only calls out are to the challenge's API, two read-only calls (POST /api/price and POST /api/availability), made only for products a request has already narrowed down to, and cached for 45 seconds. Product facts come from the challenge's product file, read once at start.

How one order flows

A restaurant buyer asks Claude for 1,000 litres of lactose-free milk by Monday. Claude signed in without a key of its own, and the buyer set its limit at €500 per order on the consent page. In a run on our test server on 14 November 2026 the usual product came to €1,560.00 ex VAT, so two rules stopped it.

  1. The buyer asks. Claude turns the sentence into a tool call. The token it presents is the mandate the buyer signed at sign-in; it travels as an HTTP header on the MCP connection, and no tool takes a token or key as an argument.
  2. Plan. plan_sourcing (REST: POST /sourcing/plan). The gateway asks the challenge API for price and availability for the delivery day and returns a few ready options: the usual product, the lowest price, a split with a substitute. Each says whether the assistant may order it alone or a person must decide, and the answer is signed.
  3. Signed offer. create_checkout (POST /checkout-sessions). The gateway runs every rule, holds the stock and signs the whole offer: lines, prices, VAT, delivery date, cut-off, the hold, and each rule's outcome. The signature (ap2.merchant_authorization) is a detached JWS, ES256, over the canonical JSON (RFC 8785) of the offer, made with Valio Aimo's signing key, whose public half is at /.well-known/ucp. Next to it, ap2.checkout_digest is the SHA-256 of that same canonical JSON. Everything an approver or agent later signs points at this digest.
  4. The rules stop it. Rule T1: an assistant without its own key may order alone only up to €500 (Valio Aimo's setting). Rule B1: the buyer's limit per order is €500. The status is requires_escalation, the stock is held for 4 hours, and continue_url carries the approval link. The card in the chat shows it as a QR code.
  5. A second person approves on their own device. They open /approve/{id}?k=… on their phone. The page asks POST /api/approve/{id}/device what to sign: the checkout id, its digest, approved or rejected, the name, the note, and each product without an allergen declaration they ticked. The device's passkey signs the SHA-256 of that object's canonical JSON, after its fingerprint, face or screen lock where it has one (the record says whether it checked). POST /api/approve/{id} sends the signature; the gateway checks it, runs the rules again (a lowered cap or a moved price shows up here, and nothing is approved on an offer that changed), and holds the stock for 60 more minutes. Anyone with the link can say no, but on this account only the device can say yes. The record keeps who asked apart from who approved.
  6. Order. Claude reads get_checkout, then calls complete_checkout (POST /checkout-sessions/{id}/complete). Price and stock are checked once more against the challenge API; if either moved, the assistant gets a fresh offer instead of an order. Claude holds no key, so Valio Aimo signs a receipt of what it accepted. An agent with its own key would instead sign the digest itself (ap2.checkout_mandate). The order confirmation (GET /orders/{id}) is signed by Valio Aimo and carries the evidence: the mandate, the offer digest and signature, the receipt or the agent's signature, the approval record with the device signature, and every rule.
  7. Peppol document and ledger. The order is also a Peppol BIS Ordering 3 Order in UBL 2.1 (GET /orders/{id}/peppol/order.xml, and order-response.xml for the confirmation). The gateway checks it against the UBL 2.1 schema and OpenPeppol's rules and the card shows the result; it is not sent anywhere. Every step above wrote a ledger entry (GET /ledger, and GET /orders/{id}/audit for one order). Each entry's hash is sha256(previous hash + sha256(canonical entry)), so an edited entry breaks the chain from there on.

Looking up many products without ordering is refused and counted against the agent: 40 different products in 10 minutes with no order (rule W4). Requests are rate limited per agent, per account and, without a token, per IP address.

In a chat, the card needs more than the model does: the QR code, the order's audit document, the Peppol XML, the mandate's limits. These travel in the tool result's _meta, which an MCP Apps host passes to the card and not to the model.

What the gateway stores, and where

In this prototype, one SQLite file (gateway.db) in the gateway's data folder:

TableWhat is in it
checkoutsEvery offer as signed, its status, the approval link's secret, and the mandate token the agent presented.
ordersSigned confirmations with their evidence: mandate, offer digest, signatures, approval record, rule outcomes.
holdsStock held per offer and its expiry. The challenge API is read only, so the gateway keeps its own holds on top of it.

kvAccounts and their order history, Valio Aimo's switches per customer, mandate settings, each agent's record (orders, asks, refusals, suspension), registered sign-in clients and refresh grants, buyers' and approver devices' public keys, allergen clearances.
runsDemo agent runs shown on the site.

Private signing keys are PEM files in the same data folder: Valio Aimo's, and for the demo accounts stand-ins for the buyer company's, the agent's and the approver's keys. Kept only in memory: the 45-second price and availability cache, sign-in codes (5 minutes), rate-limit counters, and answers to retried requests (10 minutes).

What never passes through the assistant

  • Payment data. There is none: orders go on the customer's existing invoice terms, and the offer says so.
  • The approver's private key. It is made on the phone or laptop and never leaves it. The gateway keeps the public half.
  • A buyer's own company key, when it registers one: only the public half reaches the gateway, and a JWK with a private part is refused.
  • Valio Aimo's private signing key. Only its public half is published.

Identity and keys

All signatures are ES256 (P-256). Key ids are RFC 7638 thumbprints.

KeyWho holds itWhat it signs

The mandate is a JWT the buying company signs. It names the agent, is valid only at this seller, expires, and states the limits: per order and per week, product categories, excluded allergens, what to do with a product that declares no allergens, substitutes, new products, unusual quantities, what the agent may settle alone after an order, and which proposals from Valio Aimo's own agent it may accept. Valio Aimo has its own switches per customer on top, which no buyer approval lifts: agent ordering on or off, look up only or re-order only, a cap per agent order (€15,000 by default), a ceiling per account and delivery day (€25,000), and the most a keyless assistant may order alone (€500).

Checking Valio Aimo's signature without the gateway. verify/verify_order.py takes an order's link, imports nothing from the gateway, and redoes every check: the confirmation, the mandate, the acceptance, that both sides signed the same digest, the approval and the device signature. Valio Aimo's public key is compared with the copy pinned in verify/pinned-keys.json, so a server that swapped its key would fail. --tamper lowers one price by a cent first, to watch it fail. verify/own_key.py plays a buyer that keeps its own key.

Lifetimes and cutting an agent off, as the code does them today.

  • An assistant's access token is its mandate and lasts 8 hours. The sign-in code lasts 5 minutes and works once. The refresh token has no expiry; the buyer disconnecting the assistant deletes it and suspends the agent.
  • A signed offer holds price and stock for 20 minutes, 4 hours while it waits for a person, and 60 minutes after the yes, never past the product's order cut-off.
  • Valio Aimo can suspend any agent from the control room; three refusals for breaking a requirement or a bad signature within 10 minutes suspend it automatically. A suspended agent can still look up products but cannot order until Valio Aimo reinstates it.
  • The gateway checks a list of revoked mandate ids on every call, but nothing fills that list yet. Today an agent is cut off by suspending it, and a token already issued keeps working for look-ups until it expires.

What a pilot would need from Valio Aimo

  1. Read-only price and availability for a few customers first. The same two calls the gateway makes today, against real systems, with real customer numbers (the challenge API ignores the customer number). Agents can look up and plan, not order.
  2. A named system of record for each call. Which system answers the contract price, which answers available-to-promise and cut-off per delivery day, where product facts and allergens come from (PIM or GS1 Synkka), where an order would enter (the webshop's order intake keeps credit checks, picking and invoicing as they are), and where order history and standing orders live. Stock holds would move there too, or stay in the gateway by agreement.
  3. A Peppol access point and participant ids, for customers who already order over Peppol.
  4. Key custody. Where Valio Aimo's signing key lives (an HSM or a cloud key service rather than a file), who rotates it, and how the new public key is published. Customers' company sign-in would issue mandates and gate who may enrol an approver's device.
  5. An owner for the rules. Someone on Valio Aimo's side who sets and answers for the defaults: the €500 keyless limit, the €15,000 cap per agent order, the €25,000 ceiling per account and day, the look-up limit, the suspension threshold.

What is not built

  • No connection to a real ERP, order management, PIM or webshop. Orders stop in the gateway.
  • One process and one SQLite file. Sign-in codes, rate limits and caches live in that process's memory, so it cannot run as several copies behind a load balancer as it is.
  • Signing keys are files on the gateway's disk. No HSM, no rotation: /.well-known/ucp publishes one key.
  • Stock holds are the gateway's own, not reservations in an ERP.
  • No Peppol access point and no participant id registered in an SMP. The XML validates; it has not been sent.
  • Mandate revocation by id is checked but never filled; agents are cut off by suspension.
  • Valio Aimo's side signs in with one shared operator key, not staff accounts. Enrolling an approver's device is gated by the demo account's browser session, not a company sign-in.
  • Sign-in supports dynamic client registration only, not client ID metadata documents.
  • The customers, their histories, standing orders and recipe cards are invented. What kind of product each item is was written once by a model from the product names; Valio Aimo's own product hierarchy would replace it.
  • No payment step, by design: orders go on existing invoice terms.
